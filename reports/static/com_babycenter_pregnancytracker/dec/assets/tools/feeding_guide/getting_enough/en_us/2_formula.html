How to tell how much formula your baby needs

It can be tricky to figure out how much formula to give your baby. During the first four to six months, when your baby isn't eating solid foods, here's a simple rule of thumb: Offer 2.5 ounces of formula per pound of body weight every 24 hours, with a maximum of about 32 ounces.

For example, if your baby weighs 6 pounds, you'll give them about 15 ounces of formula in a 24-hour period. If they weigh 10 pounds, you'll give them about 25 ounces in a 24-hour period.

You can also refer to our formula-feeding chart, which shows typical amounts babies eat from newborn to age 1.

These numbers aren't rigid rules; they're more of a rough estimate. Some babies will grow well while drinking less formula than recommended, while others consistently need more. Also, your baby may want a bit more formula on some days and a bit less on others.

Here are signs that your baby's getting all the formula they need:

  • Steady weight gain: They continue to gain weight after their first 10 days, and follow a healthy growth curve during their first year. (Most babies lose up to 10% of their birth weight in the first few days and then regain it by about 2 weeks old.)
  • Happy baby: They seem relaxed and satisfied after a feeding.
  • Wet diapers: They wet two to three diapers a day in the first few days after birth. Over the next few days, the amount should increase to at least five to six wet diapers a day.

If you're worried that your baby is getting too little or too much formula, talk with their doctor. They can check your baby's weight and growth and advise you about any adjustments you may need to make.
