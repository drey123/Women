How to tell whether your baby is getting enough breast milk

This is a common concern for new parents! Here are signs your baby is getting enough milk:

  • Happy baby: Your baby seems relaxed and satisfied for one to three hours between feedings.
  • Weight gain: After gaining back their initial weight loss after birth, your baby continues to gain weight. (Most babies lose up to 10% of their birth weight and then regain it by the time they're about 2 weeks old.) Expect your baby to gain about 6 to 8 ounces a week for the first four months, then about 4 to 6 ounces per week from 4 to 7 months. By 5 months, most babies will double their birth weight.
  • Wet diapers: After the first four or five days, your baby should wet at least six diapers per day. (In the first few days, when your baby is getting your thick, valuable colostrum they may only have two or three wet diapers a day.)
  • Regular poop: By 1 week old, your baby should have at least 3 to 4 bowel movements each day. Baby poop comes in different colors, and it should be loose and yellow at first. As your milk production increases, your baby may poop after every feeding.

And here are signs that your baby isn't getting enough milk:

  • Your baby continues to lose weight. If your baby doesn't start gaining weight after five days, or if they start losing weight again any time after that, talk with their doctor.
  • Your baby is wetting fewer than six diapers in a 24-hour period after the five days following their birth.
  • Your baby has small, dark stools after their first five days.
  • Your baby's urine is very dark, like the color of apple juice.
  • Your baby is fussy or lethargic much of the time. They may fall asleep as soon as you put them to your breast but then fuss when you take them off.
  • Your baby feeds for less than 10 minutes or more than 50 minutes at a time.
  • Your baby appears to have a dry mouth or eyes.
  • Your baby doesn't seem satisfied, even if feedings consistently take longer than an hour.
  • Your breasts don't feel softer after nursing.

If you're concerned that your baby isn't getting enough milk, reach out to your pediatrician or a lactation consultant right away.
