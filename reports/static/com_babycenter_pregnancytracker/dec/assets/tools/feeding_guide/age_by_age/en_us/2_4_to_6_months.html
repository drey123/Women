4 to 6 months

Babies are typically ready to start solids between 4 and 6 months, as long as they're showing signs of readiness, such as being able to sit upright with good head control.

Watch for signs, and talk to your baby's doctor about the best time to introduce solids.

What to feed

  • Breast milk or formula
  • If your baby is ready, you can start offering tiny amounts of pureed food (such as peas, squash, apples, bananas, peaches, chicken, or pears) or baby cereal.

How much per day

  • Breastfeeding or formula: 4 to 6 feedings (breastfeeding, or 4- to 6-ounce bottles)
  • When starting solids, begin with a very small amount of a single-ingredient pureed food (about 1 to 2 teaspoons).
  • Gradually increase to 1 to 2 tablespoons. If you're giving cereal, mix it with breast milk or formula so the consistency isn't too thick.

Feeding tips

  • If your baby won't eat what you offer the first time, try again in a few days.
  • Some experts recommend that you introduce one food at a time, and wait 3 to 5 days before introducing another food, so you can watch for any allergic reactions. It's also a good idea to write down the foods your baby samples. If they have an adverse reaction, a food log will make it easier to pinpoint the cause.
  • If your baby is breastfed, the AAP suggests pureed meat as a first food because the iron in beef, chicken, and turkey helps to replace iron stores, which start to diminish at about 6 months old.
