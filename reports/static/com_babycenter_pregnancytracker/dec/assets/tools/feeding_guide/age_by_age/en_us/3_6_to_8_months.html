6 to 8 months

If you haven't introduced solid foods to your baby yet, they're probably ready now (but check with their doctor just to be sure). You might choose to try baby-led weaning: Instead of spoon-feeding your baby purees, you put large chunks of soft food directly on the highchair tray or table, and let your baby grab it and feed themself.

What to feed

  • Breast milk or formula, plus
  • Fruits (banana, pears, applesauce, peaches, avocado) that's pureed or in large, soft chunks
  • Vegetables (well-cooked carrots, squash, sweet potato) that's pureed or in large, soft chunks
  • Pureed or mashed meat (chicken, pork, beef)
  • Pureed or mashed tofu
  • Soft pasteurized cheese, cottage cheese, unsweetened yogurt
  • Pureed or mashed legumes (black beans, chickpeas, edamame, fava beans, black-eyed peas, lentils, kidney beans)
  • Iron-fortified cereal (oats, barley); small pieces of bread and crackers

How much per day

These amounts are general recommendations, so don't worry if your little one eats a bit more or less than suggested.

  • Breastfeeding or formula: 3 to 5 feedings (breastfeeding, or 6- to 8-ounce bottles)
  • 2 to 3 tablespoons fruit, gradually increasing to 4 to 8 tablespoons
  • 2 to 3 tablespoons vegetables, gradually increasing to 4 to 8 tablespoons
  • 1 to 2 tablespoons grain products, gradually increasing to 2 to 4 tablespoons
  • 1 to 2 tablespoons protein-rich foods, gradually increasing to 2 to 4 tablespoons

Feeding tips

  • As your baby gets more comfortable with eating, give them a variety of foods and increase the frequency of their meals. By around 6 to 7 months, two meals a day is the norm. Starting around 8 to 9 months, your baby may be eating solid food three times a day plus a snack.
  • Research suggests that baby-led weaning isn't associated with a greater risk of choking. As long as you're watching your baby while they eat and giving them easy-to-eat foods, baby-led weaning is safe.
  • Offer a food several times before deciding your baby doesn't like it. Sometimes it just takes a little while for your baby to get used to a new flavor or texture.
  • Keep in mind that your baby's main source of nutrients will be breast milk or formula until they're about a year old.
