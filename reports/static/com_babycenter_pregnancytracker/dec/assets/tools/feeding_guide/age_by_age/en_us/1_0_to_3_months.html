Birth to 3 months

Your baby's digestive tract is still developing, so solid food is off-limits for now.

What to feed

  • Breast milk or formula only

How much per day

  • Breast milk: Most newborns nurse every two to three hours, or eight to 12 times a day. If you're pumping, your newborn may take only half an ounce at each feeding at first. By 1 month old, babies average 3 to 4 ounces per feeding – every 3 to 4 hours – for a total of about 24 ounces of milk per day. At about 2 months old, babies take about 4 to 5 ounces per feeding every 3 to 4 hours. Once your baby is 3 months old, they'll likely drink about 24 to 30 ounces of breast milk a day – usually in 4- to 6-ounce feedings every 4 hours or so.
  • Formula: Newborns typically drink 2 ounces per bottle every 3 to 4 hours. By 1 month old, they'll have 3 to 4 ounces per bottle every 3 to 4 hours. At 2 months old, they'll have 4 to 5 ounces per bottle every 3 to 4 hours. And by 3 months old, they'll have 4 to 6 ounces per bottle every 3 to 4 hours.

Feeding tips

  • Your baby may be hungrier than usual during growth spurts. These typically occur 10 to 14 days after birth and around 3 weeks, 6 weeks, 3 months, and 6 months of age.
  • The American Academy of Pediatrics (AAP) and other experts recommend that you feed your newborn baby on demand – whenever they show early signs of hunger, such as smacking their lips or rooting around for a nipple.
  • Talk to your baby's doctor if you think they need more formula or breast milk. Signs your baby's eating enough include steady weight gain and having five to six wet diapers a day.
