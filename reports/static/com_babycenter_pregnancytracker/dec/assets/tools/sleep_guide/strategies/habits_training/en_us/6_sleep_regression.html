Sleep regression

Sleep regression is a temporary period when your baby doesn't sleep as well as they used to. It happens for a variety of reasons – your baby's sleep patterns are naturally shifting; they're reaching exciting growth and developmental milestones; changes in their environment or routine have disturbed them; they're sick or teething; or they have separation anxiety.

Sleep regressions can happen at any time, but they're most common at 4 months, 6 months, 8 months, 12 months, and 18 months.

Sleep regression often passes on its own in a couple of weeks – but it sure can be rough in the meantime. If you think your baby is going through a sleep regression, here's how you can help them:

  • Stay consistent. Put your baby to bed at the same time, in the same way, every night. A soothing bedtime routine can help with this.
  • Make nighttime boring. Keep things calm and quiet in the hours leading up to your baby's bedtime so they learn that nighttime is time for relaxing.
  • Help your baby learn to fall asleep on their own. Self-soothing is an important skill your baby can learn starting around 4 months old.
  • Try sleep training . There are several methods that can help your baby learn to fall asleep on their own, including no-tears methods if letting your baby cry stresses you out.
  • Stay calm. Sleep regression is stressful both for your baby and for you. Staying calm and relaxed will help reassure your baby and hopefully get them back on track more quickly.
