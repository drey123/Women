"Cry it out" sleep training

Before you try any sleep training method, wait until your baby is physically and emotionally ready to sleep through the night – usually between 4 and 6 months of age. If you're not sure whether your baby's ready, discuss it with your child's pediatrician.

Cry it out methods suggest putting your baby to bed when they're still awake and allowing them to cry until they soothe themself back to sleep. There are two main variations of the method: "Extinction" sleep training and the Ferber method.

Here's how to try each:

Extinction sleep training

Extinction sleep training involves laying your baby down to sleep, leaving the room, and not responding to their cries until it's time to wake up in the morning.

It can be stressful to listen to your baby cry and not do anything about it, but research shows that extinction sleep training doesn't cause long-term harm to babies.

Learn more: Cry it out sleep training

The Ferber method

The Ferber method, popularized by doctor and researcher Richard Ferber, is very similar to the cry it out method. The main difference is that instead of letting your baby cry without responding to them, you can comfort your baby if they cry for an extended period of time.

To use the Ferber method, you'll follow a schedule that involves set periods of crying – for example, five minutes, then 10, then 15 – over a week, until your baby learns to soothe themself to sleep.
