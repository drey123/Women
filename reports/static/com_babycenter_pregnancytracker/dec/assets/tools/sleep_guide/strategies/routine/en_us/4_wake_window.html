Wake windows

It can be difficult to know the "right" thing to do when it comes to your child's sleep: Is it better to put your baby down for their nap earlier or later? Is it okay to wake them if they're taking an unusually long nap, or is it best to let them be?

One way you can encourage optimal naps is by understanding your baby's wake windows. Wake windows refer to the amount of time a baby spends awake and alert in between naps. The length and frequency of these windows varies depending on your child's age.

There aren't hard-and-fast rules when it comes to wake windows, but here's what you can expect by age:

  • Birth to 1 month: 45 to 60 minutes
  • 1 to 2 months: 1 to 2 hours
  • 3 to 4 months: 1 1/2 to 3 hours
  • 5 to 7 months: 2 to 4 hours
  • 8 to 10 months: 2 1/2 to 4 1/2 hours
  • 11 to 12 months: 3 to 5 hours

Here's how to make the most of your baby's wake windows:

  • Think of wake windows as a guide. Not all babies are the same, and yours might not stick to the "typical" wake windows. Use them as a guide rather than a hard-and-fast rule to create the ideal schedule for your baby.
  • Pay attention to your baby's sleep cues. Look for signs your baby is tired to figure out their wake windows – they may get fussy, rub their eyes, yawn, or look away from you.
  • Don't be tempted to extend wake windows. Keeping your baby awake past their natural wake window may make it harder for them to fall asleep, not easier.
  • Make your baby's wake windows engaging. Keeping your baby entertained and engaged while they're awake can help them differentiate between when it's time to play and when it's time to sleep.
  • Build a healthy sleep routine. Other ways you can help your baby learn the difference between "awake" time and "asleep" time include keeping things bright and noisy during the day and calm and quiet at night, and having a consistent bedtime routine and sleep schedule.
