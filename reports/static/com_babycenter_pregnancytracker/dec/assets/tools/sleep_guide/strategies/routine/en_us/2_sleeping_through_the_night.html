Sleeping through the night

For babies, sleeping "through the night" is generally considered sleeping for at least six hours straight without a nighttime feeding. Some babies can do this by about 3 months of age, or when they weigh 12 to 13 pounds.

But in reality, most babies don't sleep through the night until later: According to the National Sleep Foundation, about 62% of babies sleep through the night by 6 months – a number that rises to 72% by 12 months.

Here are some ways you can encourage your baby to sleep through the night:

  • Play during the day. Keeping babies active during the day helps them to sleep longer stretches at night.
  • Be strategic with naps. Learn and follow the cues that your baby is tired and needs to rest.
  • Have a consistent bedtime routine. Put your baby to bed at the same time every night, and have a soothing bedtime routine that gets them ready for sleep.
  • Don't rock or feed your baby to sleep. Put your baby to bed drowsy but awake. This will help them learn to fall asleep by themself, which will come in handy when they wake up in the middle of the night.
  • Teach your baby how to self soothe. Midnight wakings are normal (even for adults!), so it's important for your baby to learn how to fall back asleep without your help.
  • Give your baby a pacifier. Pacifiers can help your baby learn how to self soothe.
  • Try sleep training. Sleep training can help your baby learn to self-soothe and sleep through the night. You can start sleep training starting at 4 to 6 months of age.
