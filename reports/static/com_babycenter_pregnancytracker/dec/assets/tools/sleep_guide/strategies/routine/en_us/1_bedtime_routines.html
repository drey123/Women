Bedtime routines

A bedtime routine is an important part of teaching your baby healthy sleep habits. Research shows that children who have bedtime routines fall asleep faster and wake up less often during the night.

A bedtime routine is often good for you, too. It's a special time set aside for you to spend with your baby, something you can plan on. In the long run, routines can help your child be a better sleeper (which means you'll get more sleep, too).

Here are some tips for a successful bedtime routine:

  • Start soon. As early as six weeks, you can start following a set pattern with your baby every night. Your baby is too young to follow a sleep schedule yet, but babies thrive on consistency – so they'll still appreciate a regular nightly routine.
  • Keep it short and sweet. The younger your baby is, the shorter your routine can be. Start simple, with a feeding, diaper change, and a kiss before bed. Your routine can get longer the older your baby is – some parents have bedtime routines as long as an hour – but a 15- to 30-minute routine is perfect for many families.
  • Be consistent. Start your routine at the same time every night. It's okay if you don't do the full routine every night, but pick one or two parts to be the core of your routine that you can do every night.

Experts recommend four main components in every bedtime routine: nutrition (like a feeding), hygiene (bathtime, brushing teeth, diaper change), communication (reading, singing, and talking), and physical contact (baby massage and cuddling).

Here are some ideas for what to include in your bedtime routine:

  • Play a game. A high-energy game or dancing at the beginning of your bedtime routine can help your baby blow off steam and get any pent-up energy out.
  • Have one last feeding. Where in the bedtime routine this falls is up to you. You may feed your baby right before bed to help them fall asleep, or you may have the bedtime feeding earlier in the night.
  • Give them a bath. Getting your baby warm and clean and dry is the perfect pretext to bedtime. You don't have to soap them up every night, especially when your baby is very young, but the warm water is soothing either way.
  • Take care of business. Do one last diaper change, get your baby in their PJs, and take care of their hair and teeth or gums.
  • Read to your baby. Reading together is an incredible way to help develop your baby's language and speech skills, even when they're very young.
  • Catch up on your days. Your baby loves the sound of your voice, even when they're too young to talk back. Chatting with them about your day and asking questions about theirs is another great way to bond and develop their language skills.
  • Sing a song. Not sure what to sing? Check out our library of lullabies.
  • Play some music. Lullabies, classical music, and white noise or a sound machine can help ease the transition from awake to asleep.
  • Cuddle together. Touch is one of your baby's most basic needs. A good cuddle or baby massage at the end of the night can help soothe them to sleep.
