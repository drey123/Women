The five S's for baby sleep

The five S's are swaddling, side or stomach position, shushing, swinging, and sucking. These five things can help re-create the environment of the womb, which can be comforting for babies younger than 4 months. (The "five S's" method was developed by pediatrician Harvey Karp, author of The Happiest Baby on the Block.)

Here's how to try it:

1. Swaddle. Swaddling your baby helps them feel secure and can prevent them from startling themself awake. Swaddling isn't hard, but it's important to get the technique right to make sure it'll be safe and effective. Watch a doctor demonstrate how to swaddle or read our article with step-by-step instructions. You'll be an expert in no time!

2. Side or stomach position. Holding your fussy baby on their side or stomach can help soothe them, but only do this while holding them in your lap or arms. Always lay your baby down to sleep on their back, never their side or stomach.

3. Shush. Shushing or playing white noise mimics the sounds your baby heard in utero. Shush as loudly as they're crying, and as they calm down, lower your volume to match.

4. Swing. Support your newborn's head and gently jiggle – don't shake – your baby. Karp describes it as more of a "shiver" than a shake, moving back and forth no more than an inch in any direction.

5. Suck. Give your baby a pacifier or your thumb to suck on. Pacifiers reduce the risk of sudden infant death syndrome (SIDS), so it's okay to let your baby keep the pacifier while they sleep.
