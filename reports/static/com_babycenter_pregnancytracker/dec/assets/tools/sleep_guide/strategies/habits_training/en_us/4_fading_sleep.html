Fading sleep training

If your baby is falling asleep too late, you can try fading sleep training to shift their bedtime earlier. It can also help your baby fall asleep faster and stay asleep longer.

This method is generally a better fit for older babies and children, since it won't help your baby learn how to fall asleep on their own. It's best to try it only after your baby can consistently fall asleep without needing to be nursed or rocked to sleep.

Here's how to try it:

  1. Figure out your baby's natural bedtime. Put them to bed when you normally would. If they're still awake in 15 minutes, take them out of their room and play with them for 30 minutes, then put them back to bed. Check back on them in 15 minutes, and if they're still awake, repeat the process. The time they actually fell asleep is their natural bedtime.
  2. Put your baby to bed after their natural bedtime. On the first night of fading sleep training, put your baby to bed 30 minutes after their natural bedtime so they'll fall asleep quickly.
  3. Move your baby's bedtime. On the second night, if your baby fell asleep within 15 minutes of their natural bedtime, put them to bed 15 minutes earlier. If your baby didn't fall asleep within 15 minutes, put them to bed 15 minutes later.
  4. Keep moving your baby's bedtime. On subsequent nights, put your baby to bed 15 minutes earlier every night, until you hit your goal bedtime.
