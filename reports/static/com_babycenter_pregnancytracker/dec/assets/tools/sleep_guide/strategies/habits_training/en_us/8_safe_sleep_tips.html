Safe sleep tips

Sudden infant death syndrome (SIDS) is the leading cause of death for babies under a year old. It's a diagnosis given when a baby under a year old dies suddenly from an unknown cause, and it most commonly happens when a baby is sleeping or in their sleep area.

Here are steps you can take to reduce the risk of SIDS and make your baby's sleeping area as safe as possible:

  • Always put your baby to sleep on their back.
  • Don't share a bed with your baby.
  • Use a firm, flat sleeping surface.
  • Keep your baby's sleeping space empty. That means no crib bumpers, pillows, blankets, stuffed animals, or toys until your baby is at least a year old. If you're worried your baby will be cold, use a swaddle or sleep sack.
  • Give your baby a pacifier. Pacifier use in babies under a year old has been linked with decreasing the risk of SIDS.
  • Avoid overheating your baby. Your baby only needs one more layer than you do to be comfortable.
  • Choose a safe crib, bassinet, or play yard. Our guides on the best cribs, bassinets, and play yards can help.
  • Don't fall asleep with your baby on a couch, glider, or armchair.
  • Don't let your baby fall asleep in a car seat, stroller, swing, or bouncer. If they do fall asleep, transfer them to a safe sleeping place as soon as you can.
  • Keep your baby away from cigarettes and vaping.
  • Breastfeed if you can. Research shows the longer you breastfeed, the more protection your baby has against SIDS.
  • Make sure your baby gets vaccinated.
  • Don't use a home sleep monitor. There's no evidence these and other "safe sleep" devices actually reduce the risk of SIDS.

After 6 months of age, your baby's risk of SIDS drops significantly. If you have any questions or concerns about safe sleep practices for your baby, talk to your pediatrician.
