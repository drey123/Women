Night weaning

By the time your baby's 4 to 6 months old, they're likely getting enough calories during the day to sleep five or six hours through the night without eating. If your baby is used to waking up throughout the night to eat, though, they'll likely continue to wake up during the night out of habit, even if they're not hungry. It can take time to change this routine.

If you're not sure whether your baby's ready, talk to their doctor. They can help you make your decision based on how your baby's growing.

Here's how to night wean your baby:

  • Make sure your baby gets plenty to eat throughout the day. To make sure they get enough to eat, take scheduled breaks during the day for a quiet bottle or nursing session in a place with no distractions.
  • Start the weaning process slowly and gradually. During night feedings, nurse your baby for a shorter period of time or give them less milk in their bottle. Try to prolong the intervals between feedings by patting and comforting your baby back to sleep.
  • Offer extra feedings in the evening. This could be in the form of a bedtime bottle or breastfeeding session as part of your bedtime routine, or you could wake your baby for a final feeding or " dream feed " before you go to bed yourself.
  • Gradually eliminate feedings, one at a time. Gently soothe and comfort your baby when they wake up to eat, and explain that it's time to sleep, not eat.

If you try to eliminate feedings and your baby cries inconsolably for several nights in a row, go back to your normal routine and try again in a week or two.
